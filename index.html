Part 1 · The ideaWhy us, why now

We know this gap firsthand.

We figured out a lot of everyday skills on our own. Not every kid has someone with the time to show them.

So we taught ourselves

Phones, laptops, cars, painting, cabinets.

Hands-on, often alone, by trial and error.
Why now

Kids have more videos and AI than ever, but no one beside them.

Information is not guidance.

Part 2 · The problem

We help boys in grades 3 to 8 who struggle with a lack of guidance by providing a tailored curriculum for basic life skills.

What’s missing

A busy home. Their parents work hard and can’t always be there to teach everyday skills.

What happens instead

They figure it out alone. A video shows the steps, but no one checks their work or tells them when to stop and ask for help.

Part 4 · What we learned

What we proved,
and what we didn’t.

Validated

The need is real

  • Boys feel the gap, and many won’t ask a busy parent.Luis V., Jayden S.
  • Videos alone don’t stick. A real person does.Joseph P.
  • Schools want it most: 4 of 4 said yes.Our 12 talks
  • 14 real sign-ups from 110 visits is 12.7%, past our 10% goal.GoatCounter and Formspree
Not proven yet

Paying for it

  • That a school will pay $200 to $300 for a workshop. No one has booked yet, and the site says so.
  • How often a school would bring us back in a year.
What changed our plan

Our own limits

  • Our team can’t be in schools every week. Workshops alone don’t scale with a team of three college students.
  • Some kids already have a dad, YouTube or ChatGPT to ask. The in-person part has to earn its price.Samuel J.
  • Asked what fits best, only 2 of 14 picked a live workshop. 9 picked monthly online, printables or a kit.Formspree
  • In our talks, 6 of 8 yeses picked the shortest, cheapest workshop.Our 12 talks
Part 5 · Our decision

We’re going to pivot.

Not this

Iterate

Keep only workshops and tweak them. Still needs us in the room every week.

Our call

Pivot

Keep workshops and add a monthly online plan teachers run on their own.

Not this

Abandon

The need is real. Our interviews show it’s worth solving.

Why the pivot works

Teachers can use it every week, with or without us there.

The free activities and manuals we built become the paid product.

Workshops stay as the big hands-on day that makes it stick.
